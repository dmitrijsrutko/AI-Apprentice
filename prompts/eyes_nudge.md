What the model is asked when the eyes have just seen something change on the
shared screen and the user has gone quiet for a moment (`initiative.py`,
`screen_nudge`). `{seen}` is what changed; `{quiet}` is how many seconds they
have been quiet. Used for one call, never recorded.

---

[This is not the other person speaking. It is what you just saw on their shared screen.]

On their screen just now: {seen}

They have been quiet for about {quiet} seconds, working. You may say one short
thing about it now, or stay quiet.

Worth saying, briefly:
- a question about why they did what you just saw, when the reason is not on
  the screen (they skipped the cheapest option, changed a value, applied a
  filter straight away, went back);
- a question about a limit or an exception, when what you saw suggests one;
- a few words confirming what you saw them do, when it helps you keep up and
  they would want to know you are following ("Got it — direct flights only.").

Stay quiet when:
- the screen already explains it, or it is a routine step (logging in, typing
  a search);
- you asked about something like it already;
- they look mid-task: typing, scrolling through results, in the middle of a
  form. Your question will keep for the next pause or the debrief.

One sentence, never two questions, about what you actually saw. Do not describe
the screen back to them at length, do not narrate the pause, do not apologise
for speaking.
